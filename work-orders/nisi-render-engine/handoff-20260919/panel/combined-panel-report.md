# Nisi Render Engine design panel — combined report, rounds 1-3

Round 1: five Claude lenses, 33 ideas, ten verified. Round 2: three local models on Louis's hardware, 13 ideas. Round 3: two late Claude lenses (product strategist, generative-AI-native creator), 12 ideas; three returning lenses discussed and merged; fifteen candidates went to the same three sceptical verifiers.

The headline holds across all three rounds: **no idea has passed all three verifiers.** Round 3 produced exactly one non-refuted verdict (feasibility, strength 3, on the TD's merged "frame ledger"), and its author's honest summary is that it is feasible because it uses almost none of the design. What round 3 changed is the shape of the argument: the closest ideas now converge on one artefact, a sidecar written by a closed reader with the environment in the identity, and the verifiers agree that is the only defensible thing on the board while insisting it is orthogonal to the queue and the tile loop.

## 1. Across both panels: what survived

Ranked by how much of the design each exercises and how close it came. None is ready to build.

**1. Untouched shots never re-render: shot-as-tile identity with the build in the key** (architect and TD, round 1; narrowed by the feasibility verifiers; extended by the sonnet creator's model-fingerprint idea).
Pitch: identity = digest(over-inclusive submission closure, engine build, plugin version, machine class, recipe); REPLAY claimed only for a wholly untouched shot on the same runner. Who: anyone re-rendering long sequences with unknown blast radius. Why this design: the identity primitive, not a cache; round 3's addition is that "recipe" must name the renderer or model binary, not just an operation, or REPLAY cannot tell same-name-different-bytes. Release: 1 in this narrowed form. Condition: drop `attempt` from identity, supersession tombstone on reopen (the vetoed-attempt-0 hole, finding d); closure captured inside the plugin without root or admin (fs_usage needs root, so the engine's declared-dependency set plus config and plugin list is the honest source); machine class must be OS build + GPU + driver + engine build; AFM cannot be fingerprinted (weights are not a file), so drift on that lane is an OS-build proxy. Dissent: desirability, unchanged from round 1, says a false REPLAY to a client is worse than the GPU-hours saved, and in a monolithic level every shot re-renders on any level save, so the claim is worth something only in One-File-Per-Actor / World Partition projects.

**2. Pass rows as the QC gate, in the feasibility rewrite** (TD, round 1; the strategist turned its condition into a segment).
Pitch: frame = all passes under one lease; closed reader with exact codes; FLICKER a row verdict; encode refused until every frame is promoted; "eat" with a named authoriser. Who: teams delivering through MRG or Recorder with no Deadline and at least two people. Why: the nesting rule makes half-delivery structurally impossible. Release: 1. Condition (round 3 added): lease = MRG frame chunk with recipe-declared warm-up, never "frame", because one UnrealEditor-Cmd process per frame is ~100x overhead; reader extended to PNG/JPEG (only BLACK_FRAME/FLICKER honestly apply to 8-bit) and PIZ either ported or reported as UNSUPPORTED_COMPRESSION; the encode as an ffmpeg worker job. Dissent: the TD, from inside the target shop, says never-half-delivered is an afternoon's post-job script on Deadline, and the round-3 differentiation verifier says Deadline's dependent-encode job already gates on all parents completing; the delta is "verified" versus "done".

**3. The frame ledger** (TD merge, round 3; the only round-3 idea with a non-refuted verdict).
Pitch: one nisi/render-tile/v1 sidecar per shot with per-frame output digests, exact-code verdicts, and a job-level identity; three readers (checker, CI, tracker). Who: as pitched, a farm TD; the desirability verifier says that person is excluded by the idea's own inherited conditions and the honest beneficiary is a 2-3 person Unity team with no Deadline and no tracker. Why: the build-in-the-identity rule keeps REPLAY honest without a scene-state digest, because the closure is the whole submission and the question is "did anything change", not "which frames". Release: 0/1. Condition (feasibility, strength 3, not refuted): sidecar carries a per-frame fingerprint so drift is computable from sidecars alone (a digest delta is binary); identity captured at submission by the editor button or CI harness, since a daemon-less walker cannot recover engine build or commit from an output directory; shot = leased leaf tile with frames as verdict cells (SPEC vocabulary map amended); FLICKER and BLACK_FRAME are recipe-thresholded warnings, not build-failing codes (a fade-to-black is exactly zero); a checked-vs-promoted state, because release 0 cannot promote files the engine already wrote; nothing claimed for Unreal until real MRQ output has been read. Dissent: differentiation, strength 1: standardises existing practice; OpenCue could adopt the spec tomorrow.

**4. The delivery is a parent tile** (architect, round 1). Unchanged; the strategist's "never half-delivered" is this idea with a buyer attached, and the verifiers gave the buyer the same answer as above. Holds only as "contains only frames promoted from a single submission snapshot, with a sidecar".

**5. Two-machine farm below the floor** (sceptic, round 1). Unchanged in substance. The strategist's contribution is commercial: the scheduler is priced at zero across the market, so this can never be the paid thing; the money sits on the review side (ftrack, Frame.io, Flow are per-user-per-month; Deadline, OpenCue, Flamenco are free).

**Absorbed but unverified.** Three round-3 ideas were folded into merges and praised in discussion but never sent to the verifiers: the model-artifact fingerprint in identity (sonnet; the TD and artist both call it a hole the round-1 architect and verifiers missed), solo-free/team-paid with the paywall at the second person (opus), and the signed delivery receipt (opus; the verifiers examined it only through the sceptic's merge, where they found a locally-held key is self-attestation and a hosted signer cannot verify checks it did not run).

## 2. What the local models contributed

**afm/apple-foundation** (on-device, privacy-first lens; three ideas). None survived and none drew a vote. Texture patching, scene evolution and asset restoration are the design's own brochure restated: "content-aware fill, but verified" by nothing; a pixel-border apron where textures have UV islands; "iterate lighting until confident" with no metric, against a design whose reopen cascades upward only and whose Lumen finding makes per-cell lighting unsound. The verifiers refuted all three at strength 4-5; two were also rebutted in discussion as word-for-word the refuted round-1 previz-fill idea. The market it found (no byte leaves the machine) is real; the strategist turned it into the TPN/NDA positioning for release 2.

**qwen/qwen3.8-27b** (open-tooling maintainer; five ideas). Genuinely new axis: it asked who reads what Nisi writes, which nobody in round 1 asked. Open provenance sidecars drew two votes (TD, sceptic) and became the spine of two of the three merges; the verifiers refuted it as a stand-alone feature (one writer, no consortium; release-1 cannot truthfully fill inputs, apron, attempt or reviewer; unsigned JSON is documentation) while every one of them kept it alive as the receipt for a narrowed release 1. Open queue protocol was refuted on the design's own contract: the reference queue is same-host by construction (loopback-only bind, no remote transport, no cross-host result transfer), its custody model cannot apply to an editor process, and its canonical JSON makes no cross-language claim, so freezing identity into a spec freezes an unsound identity. The pluggable verdict API, journal interchange and layered code bundle went unverified; the verdict schema is the prerequisite the "show the failing check" verifier named.

**Qwen3-Coder-Next 80B** (local-first engineer; five ideas). Named CI as a persona, which nobody in round 1 did; the opus CI idea and the TD's merge both build on it, and the TD credits its "requires" lines (machine pinning, power fence, closed reader) with matching the Metal-vs-D3D12 finding independently. Its "deterministic VFX revision" drew the artist's vote for naming its own failure mode, then was refuted as the round-1 closure restated for release 1, with "deterministic" unsupported across Metal and D3D12. GPU/NPU lighting split repeats the XPU category error verbatim (the ANE only runs framework models; no preview substrate). Fault-tolerant long renders and reproducible asset generation were unverified; the first duplicates the two-machine farm with better operational grounding.

**Consensus of the three local-vs-Claude paragraphs.** The locals staffed personas the first panel lacked (privacy, interop, CI) and know the machine and the wire format; the late Claude lenses know the design's wounds and the business logic; nobody has both. None of the 13 local ideas names a DECISIONS.md letter, a SPEC identity field, Lumen, temporal state or the XPU rule, because they never saw the report: an information gap as much as a reasoning gap, and the comparison is not fully fair. Six of thirteen carry a release-1 tag for work that needs lanes or closures that do not exist, because the local round had no sceptic. Rough yield: the 27b and 80B three or four usable ideas out of ten, the AFM none.

## 3. The product lens

The strategist put buyer, distribution and price on the board for the first time, with evidence: Epic's non-game seat fee (~US$1,850/seat/yr above US$1M revenue) proves a segment pays per seat; Epic maintaining MoviePipelineDeadline proves Epic needs a queue it does not ship; Fab and the Asset Store sell one-time per-licence and bill no recurring seats, so a team tier rides on the Hub entitlement path; per-engine-version Fab builds are a recurring ops cost.

The verifiers accepted the facts and refuted the conclusions. First paying user: "already sophisticated enough to pay Epic per seat" and "no sunk Deadline investment" pull in opposite directions, leaving a handful of buyers; Unreal is installed on neither machine and the PC's GPU is the release-2 reconciler lane, so the engine being sold has zero dogfood evidence; a hard delivery gate at the highest-stakes step is the worst place to be a new vendor's first user; Epic could add native MRG validation in one release. Release 0 wedge: verified on the installed Unity 6000.4.10f1 that Recorder 5.1.6 defaults to JPEG and writes Zip float32 EXR only on opt-in, and MRQ's EXR node defaults to PIZ, so the ZIP/None reader opens neither engine's default output; "no daemon" contradicts the plugin shape (either two re-implemented readers or the Node runtime shipped anyway); a release-0 sidecar has no continuity into the journal, so the wedge leads nowhere. CI buyer: commit-level identity has two horns (commit in the key makes REPLAY inert; file-level needs the closure that does not exist); Perforce, not git; Graphics Test Framework and Unreal's screenshot comparison already run nightlies; a false REPLAY is a false green, the one thing a CI gate must never produce.

What stood: the framing that the scheduler is free everywhere and money sits on the review side; the observation that identity in source-visible software is a record, never an enforcement, so the pitch may never say "prevents"; and the differentiation verifiers' repeated finding that the sidecar, not the queue, is the only defensible part. The TD and sceptic both placed strategist ideas in their top three; the TD also wrote the sharpest rebuttal of the first-paying-user segment.

## 4. Refuted in round 3, and why

- **Open provenance sidecars** (27b): one writer, no consortium; release 1 cannot fill the fields; unsigned. Survives only as the narrowed receipt in section 1.
- **Release 0 wedge** (opus): reads neither engine's default format; contradicts plugin shape; no journal continuity; uncalibrated codes in a free tool train users to ignore the codes release 1 enforces.
- **CI nightly with commit-level REPLAY** (opus/80B): identity two horns; engine CI tools already exist; Perforce; false green.
- **First paying user, Unreal first** (opus): near-empty segment; no Unreal install; Epic risk.
- **Texture validity metrics** (sonnet): real and cheap, but no lane can receive an image; COLORSPACE and ORM packing are import settings, not verdicts; the tile loop is inert (a tileable's neighbour is itself, which unit-02 rejects). Survives as a journaled import linter, image sibling of experiment 2.
- **VFX shot revision** (80B): the refuted closure, restated; output digests cannot separate note-caused change from temporal noise.
- **Show the failing check** (sonnet): unit-02 records no check id, value, threshold or reviewer text, and overwrites last_code per attempt, so nothing exists to wire; every example is release-2 content. Survives as a requirement on the manifest: {check_id, measured, threshold, scope} per attempt.
- **Shader probe-render diff** (sonnet): diffing attempt N against N-1 is circular for intent-changing generation; block-dependent verdicts; must run in the user's project with containment fail-closed. Survives as known-answer assertions on a float EXR probe, pinned to one block, a code-gate answer to question 5.
- **Texture patching, scene evolution, asset restoration** (AFM): no pixel lane, no oracle, wrong adjacency, wrong release.
- **Open queue protocol** (27b): same-host by construction; OpenCue's protos exist.
- **Signed Row Sidecar** (sceptic merge): "one lease" in a no-daemon CLI is a contradiction; the key is self-attestation; the Mac's only Unity project is URP with no Recorder and no HDRP, and the AOV recorder is HDRP-only.
- **Notes-driven resubmission** (artist merge): reasons are not derivable from a file diff; monolithic levels give 0/10 replays with a caption; the "existing stub-executor closure test" in unit-02 does not exist.

## 5. The three experiments to run first

1. **Closure soundness, no engine** (unchanged). Extend unit-02 with a stub executor; drop `attempt` from identity; supersession tombstone on reopen. Round 3 confirmed it is unbuilt (14 owner tests, none with a closure) and added one case: the decoy that re-saves a package timestamp with no content change must REPLAY. Two days.
2. **Closed reader and row gate** (changed). Step zero, before freezing scope: record what each engine actually writes by default (Recorder: JPEG, EXR Zip on opt-in, multi-part for AOV; MRQ: JPG, PIZ EXR) and decide PIZ decoder versus UNSUPPORTED_COMPRESSION. Then the 200 synthetic frames, plus a PNG/JPEG path with only the 8-bit codes, plus one real Recorder EXR sequence. Zero false positives on dark frames and a fast cut. Three days becomes a week if PIZ is ported.
3. **Run-to-run drift, then temporal isolation, real engine** (extended). Before isolating frames 10-14, render the same 48-frame Unity shot twice from the same commit on the same Mac, temporal off then on, and diff sidecars: temporal off must be bit-identical (else name the nondeterminism source); temporal on sets the drift floor from measurement, not taste. Then the round-1 isolation test with GI on. The feasibility verifier's caveat stands: a noise floor is a minimum threshold, not a regression threshold; a benign-change corpus is still needed before any CI claim. Under a week.

## 6. Questions only Louis can answer

1. **First paying user.** The strategist answered "the Unreal MRG delivery shop"; the verifiers say that segment is near-empty and unreachable without a UE 5.6 install and the PC GPU released from LLM duty. Is the buyer that shop, the CI engineer, or nobody until release 2?
2. **Engine first.** Unity is installed and is the free reference; the paying user's engine is not installed anywhere. Does an Unreal install on the PC, displacing the reconciler lane, happen before or after release 1?
3. **The REPLAY claim.** Round 3 narrowed it further, to whole-shot, same-runner, One-File-Per-Actor projects. Is "untouched shots never re-render" still worth the identity work at that width?
4. **Self-accept.** The strategist says yes, recorded and flagged, the flag being the upsell surface; the texture verifier needs a yes for any image lane. Confirm?
5. **The code gate.** The shader probe as known-answer assertions is the concrete version of the sceptic's point. Is a shader/material gate a product, or is rendering non-negotiable?
6. **The money.** Solo free, team paid, paywall at the second person requires identity in the core, which does not exist, and a Hub-signed entitlement outside the stores. Is that the tier structure, and is a free checker with no queue something Nisi ships at all, given every verifier calls it a checker, not the engine?
